%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
  \headerbox{The Thomson Problem}{name=problem,column=0,row=0,span=1}{
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
The Thomson problem asks how to find the minimum energy distribution of $N$ electrons on the surface of the sphere. The \emph{energy}
of $N$ points, $p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{N}$, on the sphere is defined
as 
\[
E={\displaystyle \sum_{i<j}\frac{1}{\left|p_{i}-p_{j}\right|}}\mbox{.}
\]
Number 7 on Smale's list of conjectures, this problem remains one
of the most important unsolved packing problems, and has applications in biology and physics \cite{wiki}.
%Not just important
%in mathematics, the Thomson problem is related or equivalent to many
%other problems in biology, physics, computer science, chemisty, etc
%{[}wiki{]}. 

Known solutions have been rigorously proven for only a small number
of special cases. In general, finding a solution is an NP hard problem, and progress has relied on computational methods
\cite{wales}.
\\[-.8em]
%Using such methods, smallest known energy distributions for most points
%sets less than 1000 have been found {[}cite paper{]}.
}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
  \headerbox{Circle Packings}{name=packing,column=0,below=problem, above=bottom}{
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
   Circle packings are configurations of circles with specified patterns
of tangency. The following theorem provides rigidity and existence
of circle packings on the sphere.

\begin{namedtheorem}[Koebe-Andreev-Thurston]

There exists a circle packing on the sphere for any finite triangulation
of the sphere. Furthermore, this circle packing is unique up to M\"{o}bius
transformations.
\end{namedtheorem} 

\vspace{-.5em} 
\noindent\begin{tabular}{@{\hspace{0.0em}}c@{\hspace{0.0em}}c@{\hspace{0.0em}}}
\includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth]{images/complex_to_sphere.pdf}\\[-.5em]
\captionfont{\emph{A tangency pattern and its circle packing.}}
\end{tabular}
\\[.5em]
The actual radii for the circle packing guaranteed by this theorem
can always be found using the \emph{Circle Packing Algorithm} \cite{algor}.
\renewcommand{\thefootnote}{\fnsymbol{footnote}}
Moreover we have a feature rich software package,  $\tt{CirclePack}$\footnote{\url{http://www.math.utk.edu/~kens/CirclePack/};
special thanks to Ken Stephenson, University of Tennessee, for developing and maintaining this
freely available software.
}, for computing and visualizing the lovely results.

\begin{namedtheorem}[Thurston's Circle Packing Conjecture]

The conformal mapping of a bounded simply connected domain onto the
unit disc can be approximated with a circle packing.

\end{namedtheorem}

In summary, we have the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An essentially unique circle packing for a spherical triangulation
exists.
\item The circle packing can be computationally realized using $\tt{CirclePack}$.
\item As the number of circles increase, a circle packing analogue will
converge towards its continuous counter part  \cite{introCP}. 
\end{enumerate}
}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\headerbox{Combinatorics}{name=combinatorics,column=2,row=0}{
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
   Repelling points on a flat surface naturally adopt a hexagonal pattern. However the topology of the sphere precludes a triangulation where every vertex has 6 neighbors. Try wrapping a sheet of paper around a basketball --imperfections in your wrapping job are unavoidable.

The Euler characteristic states that there will be 12 such ``imperfections'' where a vertex has 5 neighbors. The truncated icosahedron, a soccer ball, is a classic example. Thus a combinatorial perspective may shed new light on this problem.  
}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\headerbox{Manipulations}{name=manipulations,column=2,below=combinatorics,row=0}{
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
Now it is demonstrated how manipulating the combinatorics of circle packings can be used to reduce energy.      
    A Whitehead move, or ``flip'', replaces one interior edge with another in the union of two edge-sharing faces. Such a modification does not change the number of points -only the combinatorics locally. However these local changes result in a new circle packing and thus invoke a global geometric change.
    
    A common issue with optimization algorithms is settling into a local but not a global minimum. In the figures below, two defects are separated be an area of degree six vertices. Following a sequence of flips, the defects are then combined and neutralized. Locally such defects may be difficult to fix, but the ``ladder'' bridges the two local imperfections.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\headerbox{A Discrete Model}{name=model,column=1,row=0}{
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
The Thomson problem is well studied, and there is a large catalog
of current minimal energy distributions \cite{thomson:wales}. These point sets are stored
in the interactive web applet, $\tt{thomsonapplet}$\footnote{\url{http://thomson.phy.syr.edu}, Cris
Cecka, Primary Author. ccecka@seas.harvard.edu}, which can also generate random point sets, measure the energy, and
implement a wide array of algorithms. 

To construct a circle packing model for $N$ energy points on the
sphere we did the following: 
\begin{enumerate}
\item Find the Delaunay triangulations for $N$ points using data from $\tt{thomsonapplet}$. 
\item Compute the circle packing for this triangulation using $\tt{CirclePack}$.
\item Normalize the packing by applying a M\"{o}bius map such that the
centroid of the circle centers in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ is at the origin. 
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{error }of this model was measured as, 
\[
error={\captionfont \frac{\mbox{(energy from circle packing)-(energy)}}{\mbox{energy}}}
\]
\\[-1.25em]
\noindent\begin{tabular}{@{\hspace{0.0em}}c@{\hspace{0.0em}}c@{\hspace{0.0em}}}
\includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth]{images/data_and_chart_for_printing3.pdf}
\\[-.5em]
\end{tabular}
The Figure above shows the error for minimal known $N$ distributions. The error from our experiments never exceeded $0.0062$, and as
expected the error tended toward $0$ for large values of $N$. The error introduced by the discreteness of circle packings
is limited for even very small $N$ and inconsequential for large $N$.
